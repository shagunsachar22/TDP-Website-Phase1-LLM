Practice 01 · The Jackson Triangle

Before any hard conversation — tap three corners.

A 30-second protocol. Drop the ego. Name the intent. See the other person. Then send the message.

0 / 3 Tapped
Now send the message →
Origin

Phil Jackson

NBA · Bulls + Lakers · 11 championships
Before every play, name your ego, your intent, and the other person's stake — out loud.

Jackson's huddle wasn't a strategy session. It was a presence protocol. Three things had to be named before the play began. He used it on Jordan and Kobe — the two highest-ego players in the game. The triangle works because it forces you to step out of yourself for thirty seconds before you act. Same instinct that founders need before the hard Slack message, the cofounder talk, the firing.

When to use

The founder situations this protocol was built for.

01

The hard Slack message

Before you hit send on anything that could land sharp. Tap three corners. Re-read.

02

The cofounder conversation

When you're heading into a talk you've been mentally rehearsing for two days.

03

The firing

The one moment where ego and intent both show up loudest. Slow down. Three corners.

04

The investor email

When you're writing the update where the news is bad. Drop the spin. Name what you actually want from them.

05

The all-hands

Before walking on stage to say something that matters. Especially when "what they need" is different from "what you want to say."

06

The performance review

Both directions — giving one, receiving one. Run it before the meeting, then again after.
