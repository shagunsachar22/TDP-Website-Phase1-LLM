Practice 03 · The 90-Second Recovery

Djokovic's rule: ninety seconds between any two points.

Between the bad meeting and the next one — start the timer. Don't open Slack until it hits zero.

1:30
Ready
Origin

Novak Djokovic

ATP · Tennis · 24 Grand Slams
The point that just happened is over. The next one hasn't started yet. Ninety seconds belong to neither.

Djokovic doesn't just reset between points — he treats the 90 seconds as sacred. No replay. No projection. Towel, breath, look at the strings, walk to the line. The body regulates and the next point starts clean. Founders don't get this gap by default. The bad meeting bleeds straight into the next one. The rejection email opens while the previous call is still live. The protocol is: insert the gap, on purpose, and don't fill it with input.

When to use

The founder situations this protocol was built for.

01

Between back-to-back meetings

Stand up. Walk to a window. Don't open Slack. The previous meeting has to die before the next one is born.

02

After a hard rejection

Investor passes. Customer churns. Big hire says no. Ninety seconds before you reply, post, or pivot.

03

Mid-fundraise

The day of multiple pitches. Each one needs its own clean start. The previous one is a different match.

04

After difficult feedback

Cofounder, board member, employee — anyone tells you something hard. Don't respond inside the first 90.

05

When the body says "stop"

You notice the jaw, the chest, the breath getting short. Don't push through. 90 seconds, then continue.

06

Before the high-stakes one

Final-round interview, board update, all-hands. 90 seconds before you walk in. No phone in your hand.

Then resume.

When the timer hits zero, the next point has started.

Walk in clean. Don't bring the last point with you.
