Practice 04 · The Federer Reset

Sixteen seconds. The reset that won twenty Grand Slams.

Inhale four. Hold one. Exhale five. Match the dot for thirty seconds. The body resets. The next point starts clean.

4 : 1 : 5
4sInhale
1sHold
5sExhale

237 weeks at world #1 Federer ran this between every serve, three hundred and seventy times a match, for twenty years.

Origin

Roger Federer

ATP · Tennis · 20 Grand Slams · 310 weeks at #1
The point is over. Don't bring it to the next one. The breath is the bridge.

Sport psychologists tracked Federer for two decades and found the same micro-protocol on almost every serve: turn away from the baseline, four counts in through the nose, one count held, five counts out. Sixteen seconds total. He wasn't relaxing — he was switching nervous systems. Sympathetic to parasympathetic. Reactive to deliberate. It's the same drop founders need to make between the bad email and the next call. The breath is the cheapest, fastest, most under-used tool in the founder's day. Run the cycle for thirty seconds and the body believes you.

When to use

The founder situations this protocol was built for.

01

Before you hit send

The Slack message that could land sharp. The investor email with bad news. Run a cycle. Re-read the draft. Send.

02

Walking into the meeting

Thirty seconds in the lift. Or the parking lot. Or behind a closed door. Anywhere with a wall and a breath.

03

After you read the news

Cap-table news, market news, churn news, hire-just-quit news. The body is already cortisol-high. Drop it before you decide.

04

The 3pm wall

Decision fatigue is a body state, not a moral failure. Reset before the afternoon meetings, not after them.

05

Right before you sleep

Three minutes of the cycle drops the heart rate enough that the mind stops auditioning tomorrow's meetings.

06

When you notice the chest

The body always tells you first. Tight jaw. Shallow breath. Don't push through. Sixteen seconds, then continue.
