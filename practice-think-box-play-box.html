Practice 02 · Think Box · Play Box

Annika Sörenstam never let analysis bleed into execution.

One side of a line is for thinking. The other side is for doing. Switch boxes to see what each one is for.

Behind the line

Analyze. Doubt. Plan.

This is where you draft, reconsider, run the numbers, ask the second question. The work that needs friction. Time stretches. You can sit with it.

In front of the line

Trust. Execute. Don't think.

Now you do the thing you prepared for. Same swing every time. Same trust every time. Analysis is not allowed back in. If you cross back, you've left the box.

Origin

Annika Sörenstam

LPGA · Golf · 72 wins · #1 for 60 weeks
Behind the ball is the Think Box. In front of the ball is the Play Box. Once I cross the line, I do not think.

Sörenstam drew an actual line on the grass behind every shot. Behind the line: read the lie, pick the club, run the numbers. Step over the line, and analysis was not allowed back in. Same swing every time. Same trust every time. The protocol is for any work that has both a thinking phase and an executing phase — which is most founder work, and the bleed between them is what kills you.

When to use

The founder situations this protocol was built for.

01

Before the fundraise pitch

All week is Think Box. The morning of, you draw the line. From the moment you walk in, Play Box only.

02

Sales calls back-to-back

You are not allowed to relitigate the last call during the next call. Cross the line. Reset.

03

Standup

You did not come to standup to think. You came to report and ship. Think Box was yesterday.

04

On stage

If you are mid-talk and you start analyzing how the talk is going — you have stepped behind your own line. Step back.

05

The all-hands Q&A

You prepared every answer in Think Box. When the question comes, you answer from the Play Box. Trust the prep.

06

Live writing (Slack, email)

Drafting is Think Box. Hitting send is Play Box. The two are not the same person.
