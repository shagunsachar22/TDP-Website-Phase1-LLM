# Copy Options Decision Framework

Date: 2026-05-22

## 1. Purpose

The purpose of this note is to explain how I think we should judge the three copy options for the Phase 1 website.

The main question is whether we should choose copy only basis the CPC and volume data Avi has pulled, or whether we need a wider lens.

The short answer is: CPC and volume are useful, but they are not enough.

They help us understand whether people are searching for a word, and whether advertisers are paying for that intent. But they do not tell us whether TDP can actually win that search, whether the word attracts the right person, whether the word fits the brand, or whether the page will make sense to a human founder reading it.

So I would treat CPC and volume as the first filter, not the final decision.

## 2. The Better Way To Think About This

The way I would frame it is:

> Use India CPC and India volume to understand demand. Then use brand fit, organic competition, founder specificity, content depth, and LLM clarity to decide the actual copy.

This matters because a keyword can look great in the spreadsheet and still be a bad homepage choice.

For example, `entrepreneur` has very strong India volume. That makes it useful. But it is also broad. It could mean a startup founder, a small business owner, a student, a creator, or someone looking for a business course.

On the other hand, `founder` is narrower. It has lower volume, but it carries more of the emotional and operating pressure Avi is actually writing about.

So the decision is not:

> Which word has the highest number?

It is:

> Which word gets us discovered by the right people, without making the brand generic?

## 3. Why CPC And Volume Can Mislead

High volume tells us that a lot of people search for a term. But high volume also usually means the term is generic and crowded.

Terms like `entrepreneur`, `psychology`, `burnout`, and `failure` have real demand, but they are also likely to be targeted by large publishers, health sites, wellness apps, business education platforms, and media companies.

High CPC tells us there is commercial value. But CPC does not tell us whether we can rank organically.

This is the important part of Avi's point about Forbes and other publishers. A term can have good CPC because advertisers care about it, but the organic results may already be dominated by very large sites. So if we only look at CPC and volume, we may overestimate the opportunity.

That does not mean we should ignore those terms. It means we should use them differently.

Broad terms should help shape the language of the site. Narrower, more TDP-specific angles should shape the actual page strategy.

So instead of trying to win `burnout` directly, we use:

- entrepreneur burnout;
- founder burnout;
- burnout when output becomes identity;
- am I burnt out or just tired of proving;
- founder burnout vs normal work stress.

That is the wedge.

## 4. What We Should Judge Instead

I think each copy option should be judged on eight things.

| Factor | What it means | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| India demand | Are people in India actually searching this? | This keeps us grounded in real behaviour, not just brand instinct. |
| India CPC | Is there some commercial intent? | This tells us whether the topic has market value. |
| Organic competition | Who already ranks for this? | This tells us whether we are fighting Forbes/Healthline/Isha or whether there is space for us. |
| ICP fit | Does this attract the right person? | We do not want generic wellness or generic business traffic. |
| Brand fit | Can TDP actually own this language? | The copy should build a category, not just borrow search terms. |
| Pain clarity | Does the visitor feel seen quickly? | Good SEO is useless if the human does not care. |
| Content support | Do the existing essays actually back this up? | Google and LLMs need depth, not just one homepage claim. |
| LLM clarity | Can an AI system understand and cite the page cleanly? | This matters for AI search and recommendation engines. |

The key point is that SEO should not force the brand into generic language. It should help us choose the right door into the brand.

## 5. How This Applies To The Three Options

### Option A: Entrepreneur First

Main line:

> Mental training for Indian entrepreneurs.

This is the strongest discovery option.

It uses `entrepreneur` because that is the broadest India-searchable doorway. It is probably the easiest option for Google, AI systems, and new visitors to understand.

The risk is that it can become generic if we do not quickly narrow into founders, builders, burnout, self-doubt, validation, and identity.

So if we choose this route, the homepage cannot just say "entrepreneurs" and stop there. It has to immediately say:

> For founders and builders dealing with burnout, imposter syndrome, self-doubt, validation loops, and the pressure to turn output into identity.

This is the safest Phase 1 option.

### Option B: Burnout First

Main line:

> Are you burnt out, or just tired of proving?

This is the strongest emotional option.

It enters through the visitor's felt problem. It is probably the copy that makes someone pause and think, "okay wait, this is me."

It also uses strong pain/search language: burnout, self-doubt, validation, imposter syndrome, mental pressure.

The risk is that it can pull TDP too close to therapy or mental health. We do not want to sound clinical. TDP should not diagnose the reader. It should help them understand the founder psychology underneath the pressure.

So this option works best if we keep saying:

> This is not a diagnosis. This is a founder-psychology lens on what happens when output becomes identity.

This option is probably strongest for a future `founder burnout` or `entrepreneur burnout` page.

### Option C: Founder Psychology

Main line:

> Founder psychology for Indian entrepreneurs.

This is the strongest category option.

It is the most ownable phrase. It makes TDP feel like it is building a serious category, not just writing burnout content.

It also lets us use the high-volume word `psychology`, while keeping the context specific with `founder`.

The risk is that `founder psychology` may not be as instantly searchable or emotionally obvious as `entrepreneur burnout`.

So if we choose this route, the page needs to make the pain obvious quickly:

> Most founder advice teaches the outer game. TDP maps the inner game: failure, self-doubt, validation, ambition, pressure, and the cost of turning work into identity.

This option is probably the strongest long-term brand direction.

## 6. My Recommendation

I would not choose one option in a pure way.

The best version is probably:

- Option A as the SEO doorway;
- Option B as the pain language;
- Option C as the category language.

In simpler terms:

> Lead with entrepreneur so the site is discoverable. Use burnout/self-doubt/validation so the visitor feels seen. Use founder psychology so the brand becomes ownable.

That gives us the strongest balance between search, conversion, and category-building.

The homepage can lead with:

> Mental training for Indian entrepreneurs.

Then the subhead can immediately narrow:

> For founders and builders dealing with burnout, imposter syndrome, self-doubt, validation loops, and the pressure to turn every outcome into proof of worth.

Then the rest of the page can build the category:

> Entrepreneur burnout is not just overwork. It is what happens when output becomes identity.

and:

> The Deliberate Pause is founder psychology for the part of building nobody teaches.

## 7. How I Would Test This After Launch

After launch, I would not only look at whether we like the copy. I would look at what the market is actually responding to.

In the first 30 days, I would track:

- Google Search Console impressions;
- queries containing entrepreneur, founder, burnout, self-doubt, validation, imposter syndrome;
- click-through rate on the homepage title and meta description;
- clicks from homepage to Read;
- clicks from homepage to FAQ;
- time on page and scroll depth;
- any branded searches for The Deliberate Pause or Avi Agarwal.

In the next 60 to 90 days, I would use that data to decide which supporting pages to build first.

Likely candidates:

- entrepreneur burnout;
- founder burnout;
- imposter syndrome for founders;
- self-doubt as a founder;
- validation seeking for entrepreneurs;
- failure post-mortem for founders;
- career path pressure for Indian entrepreneurs.

## 8. Final Takeaway

The copy should not chase the biggest keyword blindly.

The better move is to use the big keywords as entry points, and then win on specificity.

So the goal is not:

> Let us rank for burnout.

The goal is:

> Let us become the clearest answer for Indian founders and entrepreneurs whose ambition is starting to cost them clarity, identity, and peace.

That is where TDP has a real wedge.
