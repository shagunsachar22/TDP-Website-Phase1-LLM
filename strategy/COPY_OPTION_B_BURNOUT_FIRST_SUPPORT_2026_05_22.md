# Copy Option B: Burnout First

Date: 2026-05-22

## 1. What This Option Is Trying To Do

This option leads with:

> Are you burnt out, or just tired of proving?

This is the most emotionally direct option.

Instead of starting with the audience or the category, it starts with the felt problem. It is asking the reader a question they may already be asking themselves, but it reframes the answer.

The point is not just:

> You are burnt out.

The point is:

> Maybe what you are calling burnout is actually the exhaustion of constantly proving your worth through work.

That is why this option feels more personal.

## 3. How The Page Would Flow

This page would behave almost like a diagnosis, but without becoming clinical.

It starts by naming the felt state:

> burnt out.

Then it complicates it:

> or just tired of proving?

That second half is what makes it TDP.

Lots of sites can talk about burnout. Very few will talk about the founder who is tired because everything has become proof.

## 4. Problem Section

Suggested copy:

> Burnout is not always a calendar problem. Sometimes the calendar is only where the deeper problem shows up.

Then:

> The deeper problem is identity fusion: the startup does not just become your work. It becomes your proof. Every missed target becomes a private accusation. Every win becomes too small to rest inside. Every pause feels like falling behind.

This is useful because it avoids the generic burnout conversation.

It does not say:

> You are tired because you work too much.

It says:

> You are tired because the work is carrying too much meaning.

## 5. Pain Section

Suggested bullets:

- You are tired, but sleep does not fully fix it.
- You are successful, but the relief never lasts.
- You keep looking for feedback, then flinch when you get it.
- You know the work matters, but the work now decides your worth.
- You are not exactly broken. You are just running every decision through threat.

These lines are stronger than generic symptoms because they connect the body, work, and self-worth.

They also let us use SEO-relevant terms like burnout, self-doubt, validation, and imposter syndrome without making the page read like a keyword list.

## 6. Avi As The Guide

Suggested copy:

> Avi Agarwal knows this loop from the inside. He built CreditVidya, sold it to CRED, and then had to confront the part of ambition no funding round prepares you for: the body keeps score long before the founder does.

This is useful because the page is emotionally heavier. So Avi's lived experience matters more here.

If the page is going to talk about burnout, it needs to feel earned.

## 7. Method Section

Suggested copy:

> TDP trains the pause between pressure and reaction.

Then:

> The goal is not to stop caring. The goal is to stop letting every outcome become identity.

The chain:

1. Pressure arrives.
2. The body contracts.
3. The mind builds a story.
4. The story becomes identity.
5. The pause breaks the chain.

This gives the page a method, not just empathy.

## 8. Difference Section

Suggested copy:

> This is not symptom management. TDP does not tell founders to be less ambitious. It helps them become less fused with the scoreboard, so they can make cleaner decisions while still building seriously.

This sentence matters because it stops the page from becoming anti-ambition.

The audience does not want to be told to care less. They want to stop being consumed by the thing they care about.

## 10. Why This Option Works

This option is probably the strongest emotionally.

It is the one most likely to make someone stop scrolling because it sounds like an inner thought, not a marketing line.

It works especially well if the visitor is already in pain.

The main benefit is resonance.

## 11. Risk

The risk is that this option can make TDP sound too narrow.

If we lead with burnout, people may think TDP is only for people who are already struggling badly. It could also pull the brand toward therapy or mental-health language, which we probably want to avoid.

So we need to be careful:

- do not diagnose;
- do not overuse clinical language;
- do not make the site sound like treatment;
- keep returning to founder psychology and decision clarity.

## 12. Best Use

I would use this option if the priority is:

- emotional resonance;
- strong problem clarity;
- better conversion from visitors already feeling pressure;
- future SEO around founder burnout / entrepreneur burnout.

Even if we do not choose it as the homepage hero, I think this language should definitely feed the problem section.

In short:

> Option B is the best pain hook.
