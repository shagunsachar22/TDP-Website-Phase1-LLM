# CPC, Volume, and Organic Competition Research — 2026-05-21

Purpose: extend the India-first SEO work with Avi's caveat that CPC and volume can be skewed by organic competition. A keyword can have strong demand and commercial signal but still be difficult to win if large publishers, wellness platforms, or broad business media already own the SERP.

## Executive Takeaway

Use India volume and India CPC to decide **whether demand exists**. Do not use them alone to decide **what page to build**.

The page decision should include:

1. India volume.
2. India CPC.
3. Organic result ownership.
4. TDP's wedge.
5. Page type.

The highest-value Phase 1 move is to use broad keywords in copy, FAQ, schema, and LLM files, then compete through narrower founder/entrepreneur-specific angles.

## Why CPC and Volume Can Mislead

High volume means many people search the term. High CPC means advertisers value the intent. Neither tells us whether TDP can rank organically.

Examples:

- `imposter syndrome` has strong broad demand, but the visible organic field includes large health and media pages such as Forbes Health.
- `burnout` and `burnout symptoms` are likely to be owned by health publishers, therapy platforms, and wellness brands.
- `entrepreneur` has strong India volume, but is too broad to own by itself.
- `entrepreneur burnout` and `founder burnout` are more realistic because they combine the broad demand with TDP's sharper wedge.

## Practical Keyword Decision Rules

### Rule 1: Broad Keyword in Metadata, Narrow Keyword in Page Argument

Use:

- `entrepreneur`
- `burnout`
- `imposter syndrome`
- `self-doubt`
- `validation`
- `career path`
- `failure`

But compete through:

- entrepreneur burnout as identity fusion
- founder burnout vs normal work stress
- self-doubt as a founder under public pressure
- validation seeking as a decision-quality problem
- career-path pressure for Indian builders
- failure post-mortem without identity collapse

### Rule 2: If the SERP Is Medical, Do Not Compete as Medical

If the result set is health/therapy heavy, TDP should not copy that framing. TDP should say:

> This is not a diagnosis. This is a founder-psychology lens on what happens when output becomes identity.

### Rule 3: If the SERP Is Business-Media Heavy, Go More Lived and Specific

Forbes-style pages tend to summarize general advice. TDP's wedge is lived founder specificity:

- CreditVidya to CRED.
- Body breaking before the mind admitted the cost.
- Indian family/status/middle-class pressure.
- Founder identity fused with company output.
- Spiritual practice translated into live business pressure.

### Rule 4: If the SERP Is Spiritual-App Heavy, Do Not Say "Meditation App"

Use spiritual practice as proof, not category.

Better:

> Mental training for Indian entrepreneurs.

Avoid:

> Meditation for founders.

### Rule 5: Build the Cornerstones Around Pain, Not Method

Future cornerstone pages should be:

- `/entrepreneur-burnout`
- `/founder-burnout`
- `/imposter-syndrome-founders`
- `/self-doubt-founder`
- `/validation-seeking-entrepreneurs`
- `/failure-postmortem-founder`
- `/career-path-pressure-indian-entrepreneurs`

Not first-wave pages:

- `/sakshi-for-founders`
- `/vipassana-for-founders`
- `/meditation-for-founders`
- `/breathwork-for-entrepreneurs`

## Recommended Next Research Step

Build a SERP matrix with 20-30 target queries.

For each query:

1. India volume.
2. India CPC.
3. Top 10 organic result types.
4. Number of big publisher results.
5. Number of mental-health/therapy results.
6. Number of startup/founder-specific results.
7. TDP wedge score.
8. Recommended page type.

Suggested score:

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 1 | Bad target: high competition, low TDP wedge |
| 2 | Use only in body copy/schema |
| 3 | Good FAQ/glossary/essay summary target |
| 4 | Good future cornerstone target |
| 5 | Build immediately |

## Current Recommendation

Do not chase generic high-volume terms directly.

Use them to make TDP discoverable, then win with founder/entrepreneur specificity.

The strongest Phase 1 keyword frame remains:

> Mental training for Indian entrepreneurs dealing with burnout, imposter syndrome, self-doubt, validation seeking, career-path pressure, and failure.
